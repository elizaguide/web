SLIDE 3

🎯 The Four Quadrants: Understanding What Drives People

⏰ Timing: 15 minutes

The Core Insight: Every person is primarily motivated by one of four drivers. When you understand someone's primary quadrant, you can craft messaging, benefits, and culture that magnetizes them to your mission.

🎭 MISSION-ORIENTED

Primary Driver: Purpose & Impact

They Ask: "Does this matter?"

Motivation: Changing the world, creating meaning

% of Population: ~25%

😊 HAPPINESS-ORIENTED

Primary Driver: Joy & Fulfillment

They Ask: "Will I enjoy this?"

Motivation: Fun, relationships, positive environment

% of Population: ~30%

📈 GROWTH-ORIENTED

Primary Driver: Learning & Development

They Ask: "Will I become better?"

Motivation: Skill building, challenges, mastery

% of Population: ~25%

💰 MONEY-ORIENTED

Primary Driver: Financial Security

They Ask: "What's the compensation?"

Motivation: Stability, rewards, lifestyle

% of Population: ~20%

How to Identify Each Type in Interviews

The Magic Question: "Tell me about a time in your career when you felt most energized and engaged. What was happening?"

  • Mission: Will talk about impact, helping others, solving big problems
  • Happiness: Will mention team dynamics, fun projects, positive atmosphere
  • Growth: Will focus on learning, challenges overcome, skills developed
  • Money: Will highlight achievements, recognition, financial milestones

📝 Instructor Notes:

Interactive Exercise: "Think of your top 3 team members. Which quadrant is each one? Now think of someone who left - were you speaking to their quadrant or a different one?" Allow 2-3 minutes for reflection.

Important: Emphasize that there's no "best" quadrant - you need all types for a healthy organization. The key is recognizing and speaking to each type appropriately.

The 5-Layer Employee Brand Architecture

Layer 1: Core Mission & Values (Foundation)

Your non-negotiable "why" - what you stand for and won't compromise on

Example - Mindvalley: "We exist to elevate humanity through transformational education"

Layer 2: Quadrant-Specific Messaging (Attraction)

Mission-Oriented: "Join us in revolutionizing how 7 billion people learn and grow"

Happiness-Oriented: "Work with brilliant, kind humans who become lifelong friends"

Growth-Oriented: "Unlimited learning budget + personal transformation as job requirement"

Money-Oriented: "Competitive global salaries + equity in a future unicorn"

Layer 3: Proof Points (Credibility)

🏆 Awards & Recognition

External validation of your culture claims

📈 Growth Metrics

Company trajectory and opportunity size

💬 Employee Stories

Authentic testimonials from each quadrant

🌟 Leader Credibility

Your track record and vision

Layer 4: Experience Design (Retention)

How you deliver on your brand promise daily

Layer 5: Evangelism (Referrals)

Turning employees into recruiters through exceptional experience

SLIDE 5

🎪 The Quadrant-Based Interview Process

⏰ Timing: 10 minutes

Stage 1: Quadrant Identification (First 15 minutes)

Key Questions:

  • "What gets you most excited about coming to work?"
  • "Tell me about your ideal work environment"
  • "What would make you stay at a company for 5+ years?"
  • "How do you define success in your career?"

Stage 2: Quadrant-Aligned Selling (Next 30 minutes)

Once you identify their primary quadrant, shift your language:

Mission-Oriented

"Let me show you the impact we're having..."

Share metrics about lives changed, problems solved

Happiness-Oriented

"Let me tell you about our team..."

Describe culture, relationships, fun initiatives

Growth-Oriented

"Here's how you'll develop..."

Detail learning opportunities, challenges, skill building

Money-Oriented

"Let's discuss compensation and growth trajectory..."

Be transparent about salary, equity, advancement

Stage 3: Alignment Check (Final 15 minutes)

Ensure mutual fit and set expectations for their quadrant needs

📝 Instructor Notes:

Role Play: "I need two volunteers. One will be a candidate (pick your quadrant), other will interview using this framework." (3-minute demo, then debrief what worked)

SLIDE 6

🔄 Quadrant-Based Onboarding & Retention

⏰ Timing: 10 minutes

First 90 Days: Delivering on Your Brand Promise

Mission-Oriented Onboarding

  • Show them customer impact stories from Day 1
  • Connect them with beneficiaries of your work
  • Include purpose metrics in their dashboard
  • Assign them to a high-impact project immediately

Happiness-Oriented Onboarding

  • Pair them with culture ambassadors
  • Include social activities in their first week
  • Highlight team collaboration opportunities
  • Celebrate small wins publicly

Growth-Oriented Onboarding

  • Create personalized learning plan
  • Assign challenging but achievable projects
  • Connect with internal mentors
  • Set skill development milestones

Money-Oriented Onboarding

  • Clearly outline advancement criteria
  • Show compensation growth trajectory
  • Connect performance to financial outcomes
  • Provide market competitive benchmarks

📝 Instructor Notes:

Common Mistake Alert: "Most companies have one-size-fits-all onboarding. You're essentially speaking the wrong language to 75% of your new hires from Day 1. No wonder 20% quit in the first 90 days."

🙋‍♂️ Anticipated Q&A (30 minutes)

Q: "What if someone fits multiple quadrants equally?"

A: This happens about 15% of the time. Look for their "stress quadrant" - which motivation kicks in when they're under pressure? That's usually their primary driver. Also, people often have a dominant and secondary quadrant - speak to both in your messaging.

Q: "Isn't it expensive to create different onboarding experiences for each type?"

A: It's much more expensive to mis-hire or lose good people. You're not creating 4 completely different programs - you're emphasizing different aspects of the same experience. The content is 80% the same, the framing changes.

Q: "What about money-oriented people? Aren't they less loyal?"

A: This is a dangerous bias. Money-oriented people can be incredibly loyal when you're transparent about compensation and growth. They're often your most reliable performers because their motivation is clear and measurable. The key is honest communication about financial trajectory.

Q: "How do you handle someone who's purely transactional vs. mission-driven?"

A: You need both types! Mission-driven people provide passion and innovation, but money-motivated people often provide stability and execution. The magic happens when you have the right mix. Pure mission people can burn out; pure money people can become mercenaries. Balance is key.

Q: "What if our company culture doesn't strongly appeal to any quadrant?"

A: Then you have a culture problem, not a branding problem. Before you can market your employee experience, you need to create one worth marketing. Start with defining what you want your culture to be, then build systems to deliver that experience.

Q: "How do you maintain culture as you scale beyond 100 people?"

A: Systems and stories. You need systems that reinforce your values at scale (hiring, onboarding, performance reviews, promotion criteria) and stories that keep the mission alive. At Mindvalley, we have "culture keepers" in every department whose job is maintaining our values as we grow.

Q: "What's the biggest mistake companies make with employee branding?"

A: Promising something they don't deliver. Your employee brand must be authentic to your actual culture. If you promise growth opportunities but don't invest in development, or claim to be mission-driven but chase only profits, you'll attract the wrong people and they'll leave quickly.

Q: "How long does it take to see results from employee branding efforts?"

A: Quality of applications improves immediately (30-60 days). Retention and referrals take 6-12 months. Full cultural transformation takes 2-3 years. Be patient but measure consistently - small improvements compound into massive competitive advantages.

Q: "Should we focus on one quadrant or try to appeal to all four?"

A: You need all four for a healthy organization, but your company's core mission will naturally attract more of certain types. Tech startups often attract growth-oriented people; nonprofits attract mission-oriented. Know your natural pull, but don't exclude other quadrants entirely.

Q: "How do you handle remote hiring with this framework?"

A: The quadrants are even more important remotely because you have less time to build connection. Use video calls to read energy and engagement. Remote workers often skew growth or money-oriented (flexibility for learning or lifestyle), so adjust your messaging accordingly.
