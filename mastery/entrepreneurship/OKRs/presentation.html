What are OKRs?

OKR = Objectives and Key Results

📌 Objective

A qualitative, inspiring goal that describes what you want to achieve

📊 Key Results

3-4 quantifiable, measurable outcomes that define success for that objective

OKRs vs Traditional Goals

Aspect OKRs Traditional Goals
Scope Ambitious (60-70% achievable) Conservative (90%+ achievable)
Measurement Quantified (0-1.0 scale) Often vague
Frequency Quarterly cycles Annual reviews
Alignment Company-wide visibility Siloed by department

History of OKRs

  • 1954: Peter Drucker invents Management by Objectives (MBO)
  • 1975: Andy Grove adapts at Intel as OKRs
  • 1999: John Doerr introduces OKRs to Google (small startup)
  • 2000s-2010s: Adopted by Meta, LinkedIn, Twitter, Airbnb
  • 2020+: Widespread adoption across all industries and company sizes

Why OKRs Work

  • 💡 Clarity: Everyone knows what matters and why
  • 🎯 Focus: Limited objectives prevent scattered efforts
  • 📊 Measurement: Quantified outcomes enable data-driven decisions
  • 🔄 Alignment: Cross-functional teams move in the same direction
  • 🚀 Ambition: Stretch goals push innovation and growth

Anatomy of an OKR

Objective: "Become the #1 fitness app"

Qualitative, inspiring, directional

Key Results:

• Increase daily active users from 500K to 2M (4x growth)

• Achieve 4.8+ app store rating with 100K+ reviews

• Reduce churn rate from 5% to 2% monthly

• Generate $5M ARR from premium subscriptions

Characteristics of Good OKRs

  • ✓ Aspirational: Should feel exciting and stretch your team
  • ✓ Measurable: Quantified with clear success criteria
  • ✓ Achievable: Realistic with maximum effort (60-70% success rate)
  • ✓ Focused: 3-4 OKRs per team (not 20)
  • ✓ Time-bound: Usually quarterly cycles
  • ✓ Transparent: Visible to the entire organization

OKR-Setting Process

🔺 Top-Down (Company Level)

Leadership defines 3-5 company OKRs for the quarter

🔻 Bottom-Up (Team Level)

Teams propose OKRs that support company objectives

🤝 Alignment

Teams negotiate and agree on final OKRs that align with company goals

The OKR Quarterly Cycle

  • Weeks 1-2: Planning & OKR drafting
  • Week 3: Review & alignment across teams
  • Week 4: Finalize & commit to OKRs
  • Weeks 5-12: Weekly check-ins & progress tracking
  • Week 13: Quarter-end review & scoring
  • Week 14: Retrospective & learning session

Tracking & Scoring OKRs

Key Result Scoring

0.0 - 0.3: Off track, significant issues

0.4 - 0.6: On track, some challenges

0.7 - 1.0: Achieved or exceeded

Healthy Target Score

Teams should score 0.7 on average (not 1.0)

1.0 = goal was too easy. 0.5-0.7 = healthy stretch

Common OKR Mistakes

  • ❌ Setting too many OKRs (should be 3-4, not 10+)
  • ❌ Making them too easy (should be 0.7-0.8 achievable)
  • ❌ Confusing output with outcome (focus on business impact)
  • ❌ Tying OKRs directly to compensation (kills risk-taking)
  • ❌ Never revisiting or adjusting (world changes quarterly)
  • ❌ Lack of transparency (OKRs must be visible company-wide)

Case Study: Google's OKRs

Search Team Q1 OKRs

Objective: Become the fastest search engine

Key Results:

• Reduce average search latency from 120ms to 60ms

• Increase query success rate from 94% to 99%

• Launch 3 new search features with 10M+ monthly usage

Google credits OKRs with their explosive growth and innovation culture.

Getting Started with OKRs

  • 1️⃣ Start small: Begin with leadership team only
  • 2️⃣ Define your mission: What is your company truly trying to accomplish?
  • 3️⃣ Set 3-5 company OKRs: Make them ambitious but achievable
  • 4️⃣ Cascade to teams: Each team proposes OKRs aligned to company goals
  • 5️⃣ Track weekly: Check-ins keep momentum and surface blockers
  • 6️⃣ Reflect quarterly: Review results and learn for next cycle
