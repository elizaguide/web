There are moments in life when someone shares an insight so profound that it doesn't just change your thinking—it changes your entire relationship with reality itself. For me, one of those moments happened during an interview I was conducting with my friend Barton Scott for The Mindvalley Podcast.
I've had the privilege of interviewing some of the most brilliant minds of our time—thought leaders, scientists, spiritual teachers, and innovators who've pushed the boundaries of what we believe is possible for human potential. But what Barton shared with me that day stopped me in my tracks. It wasn't just another theory about optimization or another biohacking protocol. It was something far more fundamental: the revelation that our personalities—the very essence of who we think we are—might be largely determined by invisible elemental forces we've never even considered.
A Personal Confession

Let me share something vulnerable with you. Despite building Mindvalley into a platform that's touched millions of lives, despite writing bestselling books on consciousness and human potential, and despite having access to the world's leading experts in health and wellness, I was struggling with something I couldn't quite name.

On the surface, everything looked perfect. I had energy, I was productive, I was living my purpose. But there was an underlying restlessness, a sense that I wasn't operating at my full capacity. I found myself more analytical than creative at times, more reactive than responsive in stressful situations. I chalked it up to the demands of leadership, the natural ebb and flow of energy that comes with an intense lifestyle.

I had no idea I was about to discover that these weren't personality traits or stress patterns—they were symptoms of elemental imbalances in my body that were literally reshaping who I was being in the world.
The Blindfold We Don't Know We're Wearing
Barton opened my eyes to something extraordinary: We live in a world where we've become incredibly sophisticated about some invisible forces while remaining completely ignorant about others. We respect gravity so completely that we'd never think of stepping off the edge of a building. We understand electromagnetic fields enough to create smartphones and wifi networks. We've mapped the human genome and can peer into the quantum realm.
Yet when it comes to the elemental forces that are literally constructing our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors every single moment, most of us are walking around blindfolded. We are complex chemistry experiments, and when our elements are out of balance, we become distorted versions of ourselves—and we don't even know it's happening.
Barton's analogy hit me like a lightning bolt: Imagine I handed you a blindfold and asked you to walk in a figure-eight pattern six stories up with no railings. You'd think I was insane, right? Yet this is exactly what we do with our health and our consciousness every single day when we ignore the periodic table of elements actively running the show inside our bodies.
My Journey Into Elemental Consciousness
After our interview, I became fascinated—obsessed, really—with understanding how minerals and elements might be influencing not just my physical health, but my mental clarity, emotional balance, and even my capacity for the kind of consciousness work I'm passionate about. I decided to dive deep into Barton's approach, starting with his comprehensive hair analysis.
The results were shocking. Despite what I thought was a healthy lifestyle, despite years of attention to nutrition and wellness, my mineral profile told a different story. I was severely deficient in magnesium and had imbalanced ratios of zinc to copper that Barton explained could affect everything from my sleep quality to my decision-making patterns to my ability to access flow states.
The Zinc-Copper Revelation

Here's what really got my attention: Barton explained that elevated zinc levels can create hyper-analytical behavior—making someone more logical and systematic but potentially less intuitive and creative. Too much copper, on the other hand, can create scattered, unfocused energy where you start many projects but struggle to complete them.

As I reflected on my own patterns, I realized there were periods when I was incredibly analytical and strategic—great for business decisions but not so great for the creative, visionary work that feeds my soul. There were other times when I felt creatively inspired but struggled with follow-through and completion.

I had always attributed these fluctuations to natural cycles, seasonal changes, or stress levels. It had never occurred to me that what I was experiencing might be my elemental profile shifting based on my mineral intake, my stress levels, and even the foods I was eating.

The Hidden Famine
One of the most disturbing insights Barton shared was about what he calls "the hidden famine." Despite living in apparent abundance, we are experiencing the greatest mineral depletion in human history. Our soils have been depleted of 80-90% of trace minerals that previous generations took for granted. The organic vegetables we carefully source, the grass-fed meat we seek out, the expensive supplements we invest in—none of it can compensate for what's simply no longer in the earth.
We're like Formula 1 race cars trying to run on watered-down fuel, wondering why we keep experiencing breakdowns, wondering why life feels harder than it should, wondering why we can't seem to access the levels of energy, creativity, and joy that feel like our birthright.
This hits particularly close to home when I think about the work we do at Mindvalley. We're committed to helping people unlock their extraordinary potential, to access states of consciousness that allow them to create lives of meaning and impact. But what if some of the barriers people face aren't psychological or spiritual—what if they're biochemical? What if the person struggling with chronic fatigue, anxiety, or brain fog isn't lacking willpower or spiritual development—what if they're simply missing the elemental building blocks for optimal consciousness?

The creative soul who can't finish projects might not need therapy—they might need copper balance. The anxious high-achiever might not need meditation retreats—they might need magnesium.
